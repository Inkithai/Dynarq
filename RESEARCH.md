1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: as **simple interest** at a rate that is the **sum of the reference rate and
   at least eight percentage points**. The reference rate itself is, for a Member State
   whose currency is the euro, either the interest rate the European Central Bank
   applied to its most recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal interest rate
   from those operations; for a Member State whose currency is not the euro, the
   equivalent rate set by its national central bank. The directive also fixes which
   reference rate applies: the rate in force on 1 January for the first semester of the
   year, and the rate in force on 1 July for the second semester. Interest runs from the
   day following the contractual or statutory date for payment.

   Source 1, Article 2(6); Article 2(7)(a)(i), (ii) and (b); Article 3(2)(a) and (b);
   Article 3(3)(a).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: **No.** The directive sets a **minimum**, not a maximum: where interest for
   late payment becomes payable, the creditor is entitled to obtain from the debtor
   **a fixed sum of EUR 40 as a minimum**, and that sum is payable **without the
   necessity of a reminder**, as compensation for the creditor's own recovery costs. On
   top of it, the creditor may claim reasonable compensation for recovery costs that
   exceed the fixed sum, for example a lawyer or a debt collection agency. So 25 EUR is
   not a ceiling, and a term excluding the compensation is presumed grossly unfair. This
   is why the reminder messages in this project mention no fee at all: the amount, and
   the legal wording, are the owner's decision, not something the rules should invent.

   Source 1, Article 6(1); Article 6(2); Article 6(3); Article 7(3).

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: **No, for both.** In transactions between undertakings the creditor is
   entitled to interest for late payment **without the necessity of a reminder**, as long
   as the creditor has fulfilled its own contractual and legal obligations and has not
   received the amount due on time (unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay).
   The same "without the necessity of a reminder" wording applies where the debtor is a
   public authority, and the fixed compensation sum is likewise payable without a
   reminder. The reminders this project plans are therefore a commercial choice, not a
   legal precondition for claiming interest or the fixed sum.

   Source 1, Article 3(1) and 3(1)(a)-(b); Article 4(1); Article 6(2).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: **Not in the sources.** The directive only says how the reference rate is
   determined and which one applies in a given semester (see question 1); it does not
   state a rate. To answer, I would need the current figure from the European Central
   Bank (the rate applied to its most recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal
   rate from the most recent variable-rate tender procedure), or, for a Member State
   outside the euro area, the equivalent rate published by that state's national central
   bank. Because no figure is available here, no interest rate appears anywhere in the
   reminder messages.

   Source 1, Article 2(7)(a)(i), (ii) and (b); Article 3(2)(a) and (b).

Not one of the four questions, but the owner's note asks it and the sources answer it:
**is a 90-day payment period allowed between businesses?** Only within limits. The
period for payment fixed in the contract must not exceed 60 calendar days, unless
otherwise expressly agreed in the contract and provided that is not grossly unfair to
the creditor; a gross deviation from good commercial practice, contrary to good faith
and fair dealing, is one of the things to be considered, and a term excluding interest
for late payment is always considered grossly unfair.

Source 1, Article 3(5); Article 7(1), 7(1)(a) and 7(2).
